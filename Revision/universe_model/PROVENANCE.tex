\section*{1. The order}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{1. The order}

The author's order of 2026-10-17 (summarised; the quoted words are the author's): create "a new model of the Universe" that includes Einstein-Lovelock gravity and "the dirac16complex quantized spinor field (which is hypothesied to represent dark matter)", coupled to gravity "using a non-trivial canonical spin connection", with "new CRITICAL interactions" of these fields with photons, neutrinos, electrons, up and down quarks and the $W$ and $Z$ bosons, "do NOT include the Higgs particle"; able to model the CMB, the CNB and BAO, so that the metric must be "very general, but perhaps expressible as a power series of terms of decreasing magnitude"; new phenomenological interactions "consistent with qcd and electro-weak models"; and, CRITICALLY, mass is to be given to all non-massless particles (all except the graviton, the photon "and perhaps the quanta of the dirac16complex field") "by appropriate wave function dependence on the space coordinate x0 (either explicit, derived, or assumed), which must be periodic in x0 with period $\pi/(6H)$", with the uncertainty relation $\Delta x_0\,\Delta p_{x_0} \geq \hbar/2$ as the reason. Then: push the model with provenance (.md and .pdf), compute the coupled field equations, compute an accurate approximate solution, document each (.md and .pdf), and complete the open stages.

The mass-from-$x_0$ idea and the periodicity requirement are the AUTHOR's. The assembly of the model, the choices labelled ASSUMED and INVENTED in \texttt{MODEL.\allowbreak{}md}, the derivations and the numerics are by Claude Opus 5.5 working in this repository.

\section*{5. Computations and tools}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{5. Computations and tools}

\begin{itemize}
\item Symbolic: Python 3.14.5, sympy 1.14.0 (\texttt{field\_\allowbreak{}equations/\allowbreak{}derive\_\allowbreak{}field\_\allowbreak{}equations.\allowbreak{}py}).

\item The analysis (1.2): \texttt{analysis/\allowbreak{}x0\_\allowbreak{}mass\_\allowbreak{}analysis.\allowbreak{}py} (sympy, numpy, matplotlib; about 30 s) and \texttt{analysis/\allowbreak{}dark\_\allowbreak{}validity.\allowbreak{}py} (numpy, matplotlib; about 3 s), which also writes the tables of \texttt{ANALYSIS.\allowbreak{}md} section 12.
\item Every calculation runs well within the limit of 423 s per calculation of this project.
\end{itemize}
