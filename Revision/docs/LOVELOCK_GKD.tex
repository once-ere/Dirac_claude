\begin{abstract}
This document records the computation of \texttt{Revision\allowbreak{}/gkd\_lov\allowbreak{}elock}: the generalized Kronecker delta of the author's notebook, re-implemented in pure Rust as the function GKD, and the three nonzero Lovelock tensors $P_{(1)}$, $P_{(2)}$, $P_{(3)}$ of Lovelock's equation (4.38) for the corrected primordial metric of 2026-10-16 (version 2) of signature (4,4), in which 3-space inflates as $e^{a_4(x_4)}$ while the hidden direction $x_0$ and the three extra times $x_5, x_6, x_7$ share the deflating scale factor $e^{-a_4(x_4)}b_4(x_4)^{1/2}$, for every real $z = 6Hx_0$. Every component of $P_{(k)}$ is exact: a polynomial in $H$, $a_4'$, $a_4''$, $u = b_4'/b_4$, $v = u'$ and $k = e^{2a_4}/b_4$. Every $P_{(k)}$ is diagonal, free of $x_0$, with equal components in the three 3-space directions and equal components in the hidden direction and the three extra times; $P_{(4)} = 0$ identically in eight dimensions. The Rust program checks 27 identities (27 of 27 pass); GKD equals the author's determinant on all 16,777,216 pairs of index lists of length 4 and on 200,000 pseudo-random pairs of each length 5 to 9. Two independent verifications agree with every component: a Wolfram Language check that evaluates the author's definition verbatim (29 of 29 checks pass) and a sympy checker that shares no code with the Rust program (56 of 56 checks pass). The field equations for $a_4$ and $b_4$ use these tensors with the normalisation $E_{(k)} = -P_{(k)}/2^{k+1}$, $E_{(1)} = G$; with the author's Lovelock weights they give, on the history $a_4 = Hx_4$, $b_4 = b_4(0)e^{(4/3)Hx_4}$, exactly the source that this history needs, a polynomial in $k$. The author's stored outputs belong to the withdrawn metric of 2026-10-14; they are compared with the frozen record of that metric (\texttt{Revision\allowbreak{}/gkd\_lov\allowbreak{}elock/co\allowbreak{}mparison\allowbreak{}/}; 80 checks: 75 PASS, 0 FAIL, 5 NOT-AVAILABLE): the author's metric, Ricci scalar and all 64 components of the author's Einstein tensor equal that record exactly under a function mapping stated before the comparison; no author output exists for the corrected metric, and in the two notebooks of the author in the repository a keyword scan finds no stored output of the Lovelock tensors of order $k = 2$ and $k = 3$. Not established here: any solution of the field equations, anything at the points $\sin z\cos z = 0$ where the metric degenerates, and agreement with any Lovelock tensor of order $k = 2$ or $k = 3$ computed by the author.

\end{abstract}

\section{The generalized Kronecker delta}

\subsection{The author's definition}

The cell stored as \texttt{\detokenize{In[87]}} in the author's notebook \texttt{Generali\allowbreak{}zed \_Kro\allowbreak{}necker\_D\allowbreak{}elta\_4+4\allowbreak{}.nb} (the author's \texttt{\detokenize{In[54]}}) defines

\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small]
kδ[lower_, upper_] /; Length[lower] == Length[upper] := Det[Outer[delta, lower, upper]]
\end{Verbatim}

For lists $l = (l_1, \dots, l_p)$ and $u = (u_1, \dots, u_p)$ the matrix \texttt{Outer[de\allowbreak{}lta, low\allowbreak{}er, uppe\allowbreak{}r]} has the entries $M_{ij} = \delta(l_i, u_j)$ (1 if $l_i = u_j$, otherwise 0), and

\[
\delta^{u_1\cdots u_p}_{l_1\cdots l_p} = \det\big[\delta(l_i, u_j)\big]_{i,j=1}^{p} .
\]

For lists of different length the definition does not apply and the expression stays unevaluated. Because $\det M = \det M^T$, exchanging the two lists does not change the value.

\subsection{GKD and the proof that it equals the definition}

\textbf{Theorem (GKD).} For lists of equal length $p$, $\det M = 0$ if two lower indices are equal, if two upper indices are equal, or if some lower index is not among the upper indices; otherwise $\det M = \mathrm{sign}(\sigma)$, where $\sigma$ is the permutation with $l_i = u_{\sigma(i)}$.

Proof (the comment of \texttt{Revision\allowbreak{}/gkd\_lov\allowbreak{}elock/co\allowbreak{}de/src/g\allowbreak{}kd.rs}). (a) Two equal lower indices give two equal rows of $M$, so $\det M = 0$. (b) Two equal upper indices give two equal columns, so $\det M = 0$. (c) A lower index $l_i$ that is not among the upper indices gives a zero row $i$, so $\det M = 0$. (d) Otherwise both lists consist of $p$ distinct indices and every $l_i$ equals exactly one $u_{\sigma(i)}$; $M$ has a single 1 in each row $i$, in column $\sigma(i)$, so it is the permutation matrix of $\sigma$, and in the Leibniz formula only the term of $\sigma$ survives: $\det M = \mathrm{sign}(\sigma)$. QED.

\texttt{gkd.rs::\allowbreak{}GKD} implements cases (a) to (d) with a position table and counts the inversions of $\sigma$: $O(p^2)$ integer operations ($p \le 64$) instead of a $p \times p$ determinant. With more than eight indices over eight values two indices of a list are always equal (pigeonhole), so GKD is 0. \texttt{gkd.rs::\allowbreak{}kdelta\_d\allowbreak{}et} is a literal transcription of the definition (the Leibniz determinant of the 0/1 matrix, permutations by Heap's algorithm) used only to test GKD.

\section{The Lovelock tensors}

\subsection{Lovelock's equation (4.38)}

The cell stored as \texttt{\detokenize{In[101]}} in the author's notebook (the author's \texttt{\detokenize{In[68]}}) is a picture of Lovelock's equation (4.38) (Lovelock and Rund), rendered to \texttt{Revision\allowbreak{}/gkd\_lov\allowbreak{}elock/re\allowbreak{}sults/no\allowbreak{}tebook-i\allowbreak{}n68-imag\allowbreak{}e.png} and read from that picture. In $n$ dimensions, with arbitrary constants $\alpha_{(k)}$ and $\lambda$ and $m = n/2$ for even $n$,

\[
A^{lh} = \sqrt{g}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{(k)}\,g^{jl}\,\delta^{hh_1\cdots h_{2k}}_{jj_1\cdots j_{2k}}\,R^{j_1j_2}{}_{h_1h_2}\cdots R^{j_{2k-1}j_{2k}}{}_{h_{2k-1}h_{2k}} + \lambda\sqrt{g}\,g^{lh} .
\]

For the author's metric $n = 8$ and $m = 4$, so the sum runs over $k = 1, 2, 3$; $\sqrt{g}$ means $\sqrt{|\det g|} = e^{-a_4}b_4^2|\cot z|(\sin^2z)^{1/4}$ (section 2.1).

\subsection{Definitions and index conventions of this record}

For $k = 1, 2, 3$ (\texttt{lovelock\allowbreak{}-tensors\allowbreak{}.json}, key \texttt{definiti\allowbreak{}on}):

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{(k)}{}^h{}_j &= \delta^{hh_1\cdots h_{2k}}_{jj_1\cdots j_{2k}}\,R^{j_1j_2}{}_{h_1h_2}\cdots R^{j_{2k-1}j_{2k}}{}_{h_{2k-1}h_{2k}} \\
&= \sum \mathrm{GKD}\big([j,j_1,\dots,j_{2k}],[h,h_1,\dots,h_{2k}]\big)\prod_{i=1}^{k}R^{j_{2i-1}j_{2i}}{}_{h_{2i-1}h_{2i}} ,\\
L_{(k)} &= \delta^{h_1\cdots h_{2k}}_{j_1\cdots j_{2k}}\,R^{j_1j_2}{}_{h_1h_2}\cdots R^{j_{2k-1}j_{2k}}{}_{h_{2k-1}h_{2k}} ,\\
A_{(k)}{}^{lh} &= \sqrt{|g|}\,g^{jl}\,P_{(k)}{}^h{}_j ,
\end{aligned}
\]

summed over all repeated indices. Conventions, stated once:

\begin{itemize}
\item The lower list of GKD is $[j, j_1, \dots, j_{2k}]$ (the lower indices of $\delta$, which carry the UPPER index pairs of the curvature factors) and the upper list is $[h, h_1, \dots, h_{2k}]$; in the code (\texttt{lovelock\allowbreak{}.rs::dfs}) the upper pair $a, b$ of a factor $R^{ab}{}_{cd}$ joins the lower row of $\delta$ and the lower pair $c, d$ joins the upper row. The field-equations branch writes the lists in the other order; by section 3.1 the value is the same.
\item $P_{(k)}$ is stored with its first index up and its second down (\texttt{P1\_mixed\allowbreak{}\_up\_h\_do\allowbreak{}wn\_j}, \texttt{P2\_mixed\allowbreak{}\_up\_h\_do\allowbreak{}wn\_j}, \texttt{P3\_mixed\allowbreak{}\_up\_h\_do\allowbreak{}wn\_j}); $A_{(k)}$ with both indices up (\texttt{A1\_contr\allowbreak{}avariant\allowbreak{}\_l\_h}, \texttt{A2\_contr\allowbreak{}avariant\allowbreak{}\_l\_h}, \texttt{A3\_contr\allowbreak{}avariant\allowbreak{}\_l\_h}). For the diagonal metric $A_{(k)}{}^{lh} = \sqrt{|g|}\;g^{ll}\,P_{(k)}{}^h{}_l$.
\item In this notation equation (4.38) reads $A^{lh} = \sum_{k=1}^{3}\alpha_{(k)}A_{(k)}{}^{lh} + \lambda\sqrt{|g|}\,g^{lh}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Normalisations}

$P_{(k)}$ carries the normalisation of the $\delta$ sum. The usual normalised Lovelock tensors are (\texttt{lovelock\allowbreak{}-tensors\allowbreak{}.json}, key \texttt{normalis\allowbreak{}ationNot\allowbreak{}e}; \texttt{lovelock\allowbreak{}.rs::usu\allowbreak{}al\_norma\allowbreak{}lisation})

\[
E_{(k)}{}^h{}_j = -\frac{P_{(k)}{}^h{}_j}{2^{k+1}},\qquad E_{(1)} = G ,
\]

with the following exact relations, derived in the docstring of \texttt{Revision\allowbreak{}/gkd\_lov\allowbreak{}elock/ve\allowbreak{}rificati\allowbreak{}on/check\allowbreak{}\_loveloc\allowbreak{}k\_gkd.py} by the Laplace expansion of the determinant, $P_{(k)}{}^h{}_j = \delta^h_jL_{(k)} - 2k\,\delta^{h_1\cdots h_{2k}}_{jj_2\cdots j_{2k}}R^{hj_2}{}_{h_1h_2}\cdots$, and verified for this metric:

\begin{itemize}
\item $k = 1$: $P_{(1)} = 2R\,\delta - 4\,\mathrm{Ric} = -4G$ and $L_{(1)} = 2R$ (\texttt{k1\_equal\allowbreak{}s\_minus\_\allowbreak{}4\_einste\allowbreak{}in}, \texttt{L1\_equal\allowbreak{}s\_2R}).
\item $k = 2$: $P_{(2)} = -8\mathcal{H}$, with the Gauss-Bonnet (Lanczos) tensor $\mathcal{H}^h{}_j = 2(RR^h{}_j - 2R^h{}_aR^a{}_j - 2R^{ha}{}_{jb}R^b{}_a + R^{habc}R_{jabc}) - \frac12\delta^h_j(R^2 - 4R_{ab}R^{ab} + R_{abcd}R^{abcd})$, and $L_{(2)} = 4(R^2 - 4R^a{}_bR^b{}_a + R^{ab}{}_{cd}R^{cd}{}_{ab})$ (\texttt{k2\_equal\allowbreak{}s\_minus\_\allowbreak{}8\_gauss\_\allowbreak{}bonnet}, \texttt{L2\_equal\allowbreak{}s\_4\_gaus\allowbreak{}s\_bonnet}, \texttt{gauss\_bo\allowbreak{}nnet\_ind\allowbreak{}ex\_place\allowbreak{}ment}).
\item $k = 3$: $L_{(3)} = 8(2T_1 + 8T_2 + 24T_3 + 3T_4 + 24T_5 + 16T_6 - 12T_7 + T_8)$ with the cubic invariants $T_1 = R^{ab}{}_{cd}R^{cd}{}_{ef}R^{ef}{}_{ab}$, $T_2 = R^{ab}{}_{cd}R^{ce}{}_{bf}R^{df}{}_{ae}$, $T_3 = R^{ab}{}_{cd}R^{cd}{}_{be}R^e{}_a$, $T_4 = R\,R^{ab}{}_{cd}R^{cd}{}_{ab}$, $T_5 = R^{ab}{}_{cd}R^c{}_aR^d{}_b$, $T_6 = R^a{}_bR^b{}_cR^c{}_a$, $T_7 = R\,R^a{}_bR^b{}_a$ and $T_8 = R^3$ (\texttt{L3\_equal\allowbreak{}s\_8\_cubi\allowbreak{}c\_lovelo\allowbreak{}ck\_densi\allowbreak{}ty}).
\item The constants are also derived, not only assumed: from literal $\delta$ sums on random algebraic curvature tensors, $P_{(1)}/G$ takes the single value $-4$ in $d = 4$, $P_{(2)}/\mathcal{H}$ the single value $-8$ in $d = 5$, and in $d = 6$ the 9 x 8 system of nine random tensors has rank 8 with the unique solution c = [16, 64, 192, 24, 192, 128, -96, 8] = 8 x [2, 8, 24, 3, 24, 16, -12, 1] (\texttt{normalis\allowbreak{}ation\_P1\allowbreak{}\_derived\allowbreak{}\_minus\_4}, \texttt{normalis\allowbreak{}ation\_P2\allowbreak{}\_derived\allowbreak{}\_minus\_8}, \texttt{normalis\allowbreak{}ation\_L3\allowbreak{}\_cubic\_d\allowbreak{}erived}).
\item Trace identity: $\sum_hP_{(k)}{}^h{}_h = (n - 2k)L_{(k)} = (8 - 2k)L_{(k)}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why the series stops at k = 3}

$P_{(4)}$ would need $\delta$ with $2\cdot4 + 1 = 9$ upper and 9 lower indices over eight values: two indices of each list are equal, so every term vanishes and $P_{(4)} = 0$ identically (\texttt{k4\_tenso\allowbreak{}r\_vanish\allowbreak{}es}, \texttt{k4\_terms\allowbreak{}\_vanish\_\allowbreak{}literall\allowbreak{}y}, \texttt{gkd\_nine\allowbreak{}\_indices\allowbreak{}\_in\_eigh\allowbreak{}t\_dimens\allowbreak{}ions\_van\allowbreak{}ish}; \texttt{lovelock\allowbreak{}-tensors\allowbreak{}.json} key \texttt{\detokenize{k4}}: ``identically zero (GKD of 9 indices in 8 dimensions)''). Equation (4.38) stops at $k = m - 1 = 3$, so $k = 1, 2, 3$ is the complete series in eight dimensions.

\section{What is established and what is not}

\subsection{Established}

\begin{enumerate}
\item GKD equals the author's definition \texttt{kδ} for all index lists of equal length (theorem of section 3.2, with its proof); confirmed exhaustively up to length 4 (Rust) and length 3 (Wolfram, sympy) and on pseudo-random samples up to length 9.
\item For the corrected metric, for every real $z$ with $\sin z\cos z \neq 0$, with the conventions of sections 2.2 and 4.2: the 21 nonzero Christoffel symbols, the 112 nonzero $R^{ab}{}_{cd}$, the Ricci and Einstein tensors and the Lovelock tensors $P_{(k)}$, $A_{(k)}$ and scalars $L_{(k)}$, $k = 1, 2, 3$, exactly as listed in section 6 and in \texttt{lovelock\allowbreak{}-tensors\allowbreak{}.json}; three programs that share no code for the sums agree on every component.
\item $P_{(1)} = -4G$, $P_{(2)} = -8\mathcal{H}$ (Gauss-Bonnet), $L_{(1)} = 2R$, $L_{(2)} = 4\,GB$, $L_{(3)} = 8(2T_1 + \dots + T_8)$; the trace identities; $\nabla_hP_{(k)}{}^h{}_j = 0$; the symmetry of $A_{(k)}$; $P_{(4)} = 0$ in eight dimensions.
\item The structure: $R^{ab}{}_{cd}$ is sectional and free of $x_0$; every $P_{(k)}$ is diagonal and free of $x_0$, with $x_1 = x_2 = x_3$ and $x_0 = x_5 = x_6 = x_7$; it depends only on $H$, $a_4'$, $a_4''$, $u = b_4'/b_4$, $v = u'$ and $k = e^{2a_4}/b_4$, and the $x_4$ components contain no $a_4''$.
\item Agreement with the author's stored outputs of the earlier notebook (section 8.2; the frozen record of the withdrawn metric of 2026-10-14, at $b_4 = 1$): under the mapping stated before the comparison, the author's metric \texttt{\detokenize{Out[235]}}, Ricci scalar \texttt{\detokenize{Out[535]}} and all 64 components of the Einstein tensor \texttt{\detokenize{Out[536]}} (raised with the author's own metric) equal those of that record exactly, and hence its $P_{(1)}$ and $L_{(1)}$ equal $-4$ times the author's Einstein tensor and 2 times the author's Ricci scalar; of the 80 checks 75 pass, none fails and 5 are NOT-AVAILABLE.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Not established}

\begin{enumerate}
\item No solution: this record gives the left-hand sides of the field equations only. Which $a_4$, which $b_4$ and which sources solve them is not part of it (section 9).
\item The domain: every real $z$ with $\sin z\cos z \neq 0$. Nothing is computed at the folds $\cos z = 0$, where $g_{00} = 0$ and $\det g = 0$, or at the points $\sin z = 0$, where the metric is singular.
\item No comparison for $k = 2$ and $k = 3$: the author's files that were read hold no stored output of $P_{(2)}$, $P_{(3)}$, $L_{(2)}$ or $L_{(3)}$, nor of the Christoffel symbols, the Riemann tensor or the Ricci tensor (section 8.2). Agreement with the author's own Lovelock tensors of order $k = 2$ and $k = 3$ is therefore not established. For the corrected metric no output of the author exists. The notebooks in which, by the main notebook's text, the author calculates the Einstein-Lovelock tensors were not read; the limits of the comparison that was made are listed in section 8.3.
\item For $k = 3$ the tensor $P_{(3)}$ is verified by three independent recomputations, the trace, the divergence and the symmetry, and the scalar $L_{(3)}$ against the classical cubic density; it is not compared with an independent closed formula for the third-order Lovelock tensor. $E_{(k)} = -P_{(k)}/2^{k+1}$ is a normalisation convention, which equals the Einstein tensor for $k = 1$ and the Gauss-Bonnet tensor for $k = 2$.
\item The unpruned sums are exact for $k = 1, 2$ (sympy) and numerical at one point (Rust); for $k = 3$ the skipped terms are confirmed by a random sample (sympy) and by the theorem of section 3.2.
\item Only Wolfram Language 15.0.1 and the toolchains named in the provenance files were used.
\item No physical interpretation is attached to the components here; in particular nothing is said about the sign of an energy or about the stability of a solution.
\end{enumerate}
