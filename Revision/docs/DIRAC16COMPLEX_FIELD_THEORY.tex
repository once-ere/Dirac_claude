\section{The field dirac16complex: Clifford algebra, Pin(4,4) and Spin(4,4)}

dirac16complex is a field $\Psi$ with 16 complex anticommuting components $\Psi_A(x)$, $A = 1, \dots, 16$ (elements of a Grassmann algebra; after quantisation, operators with canonical anticommutators, section 11). It transforms under a 16-dimensional irreducible representation of Pin(4,4), the double cover of O(4,4); for the transformations of determinant 1 it transforms under the direct sum of two inequivalent irreducible 8-dimensional representations of Spin(4,4), the double cover of SO(4,4). These representation facts are proved exactly below for the author's gamma matrices.

The gamma matrices are the author's real $16\times16$ matrices T16, rebuilt by Revision code from the author's formulas (the tau matrices of the notebook) in WolframScript and, independently, in Python, in the notebook's frame order, which is the order of the author's coordinates (SPEC section 2): $\gamma^{(a)} = $ T16[a] for $a = 0, \dots, 7$ ($\gamma^{(0)}$ of the hidden direction, $\gamma^{(4)}$ of the time) (\texttt{T16\_bloc\allowbreak{}k\_form}, \texttt{coordina\allowbreak{}te\_map}; the two constructions agree entry by entry, \texttt{fixture\_\allowbreak{}comparis\allowbreak{}on\_gamma\allowbreak{}s\_json}). \section{Non-triviality [1]}

\textbf{Theorem 1 (non-triviality [1]).} Hypotheses: the metric of section 2 with $H > 0$ and $0 < z < \pi/2$; $a_4$ and $b_4 > 0$ any twice differentiable functions of $x_4$ (constant ones included); real $m$ and $\lambda$ (or any real potential $U$); the canonical spin connection of the diagonal vielbein; $\Psi$ any dirac16complex configuration (Grassmann-valued components) that is not identically zero. Then:

\begin{enumerate}
\item in the diagonal vielbein the Euler-Lagrange operator contains the gravitational term $\gamma^\mu D_\mu\Psi - \gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\Psi = \gamma^\mu\Omega_\mu\Psi = G\Psi$ with $G = 3H\gamma^{(0)} + \tfrac34u\,\gamma^{(4)}$; $G$ is never zero (its $\gamma^{(0)}$ coefficient is $3H$), $G^2 = \big(9H^2 - \tfrac{9}{16}u^2\big)\,I_{16}$, so $G\Psi \neq 0$ wherever $u^2 \neq 16H^2$ (on the background history $u = \tfrac43H$ and $G^2 = 8H^2I_{16}$), while where $|u| = 4H$ the matrix $G = 3H(\gamma^{(0)} \pm \gamma^{(4)})$ has rank 8 and annihilates an 8-dimensional subspace (this value depends on the frame and on the field variables, see the Remarks);
\item per direction, the $a_4'$ terms of the time direction of the three inflating and the three deflating directions cancel ($\frac32 a_4' - \frac32 a_4' = 0$), the $b_4'$ terms of the three extra times add ($3 \cdot \frac{u}{4} = \tfrac34u$), and the hidden-direction terms of all six directions add ($6 \cdot \frac{H}{2} = 3H$);
\item in addition the vielbein enters every derivative term, $\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu = \epsilon_s\gamma^{(i)}\partial_i + \gamma^{(4)}\partial_4 + \epsilon_t\gamma^{(t)}\partial_t + \tan z\,\gamma^{(0)}\partial_0$, none of whose factors is identically 1; because of the cancellation in 2, $a_4$ enters the field equations through the factors $e^{\mp a_4}$, and $b_4$ through $b_4^{-1/2}$ in $\epsilon_t$ and through $\tfrac34u\,\gamma^{(4)}$;
\item all gravitational terms vanish together only in flat 4+4 space: $\Omega_\mu = 0$ for every $\mu$ if and only if $a_4' = 0$, $b_4' = 0$ and $H = 0$, and $H = 0$ lies outside the family (a degenerate limit); no choice of local frame removes $\Omega_\mu$, because its curvature is the Riemann tensor and $R^{x_0}{}_{x_0} = -6H^2 \neq 0$: the metric is never flat for $H > 0$.
\end{enumerate}

(b) Contracting with $\gamma^\mu = \gamma^{(\mu)}/f_\mu$ and using $(\gamma^{(i)})^2 = 1$, $(\gamma^{(t)})^2 = -1$ gives the per-direction values of section 4: the $\gamma^{(4)}$ coefficients are $+\frac12 a_4'$ for each of the three inflating and $-\frac12 a_4' + \frac14u$ for each of the three deflating directions, the $\gamma^{(0)}$ coefficients are $\frac12 H$ for all six; hence $\gamma^\mu\Omega_\mu = G$, independent of $a_4$ and $x_0$ (\texttt{gammaOme\allowbreak{}ga\_per\_d\allowbreak{}irection}, \texttt{gammaOme\allowbreak{}ga\_total}, \texttt{a4\_time\_\allowbreak{}terms\_ca\allowbreak{}ncel\_b4\_\allowbreak{}terms\_ad\allowbreak{}d}, \texttt{gamma\_mu\allowbreak{}\_Omega\_m\allowbreak{}u\_equals\allowbreak{}\_3H\_gamm\allowbreak{}a\_x0\_plu\allowbreak{}s\_b4\_ter\allowbreak{}m}). The same value follows from the divergence form of section 4 (\texttt{gammaOme\allowbreak{}ga\_diver\allowbreak{}gence\_fo\allowbreak{}rm}, \texttt{divergen\allowbreak{}ce\_of\_sq\allowbreak{}rtg\_gamm\allowbreak{}a}). (c) $\gamma^{(0)}$ and $\gamma^{(4)}$ anticommute, $(\gamma^{(0)})^2 = I_{16}$ and $(\gamma^{(4)})^2 = -I_{16}$, so $G^2 = (9H^2 - \tfrac{9}{16}u^2)I_{16}$; $G\Psi = 0$ implies $G^2\Psi = 0$ and therefore $\Psi = 0$ wherever $u^2 \neq 16H^2$; where $|u| = 4H$, $G$ has rank 8 (\texttt{gravity\_\allowbreak{}term\_squ\allowbreak{}are}, \texttt{nontrivi\allowbreak{}ality\_1\_\allowbreak{}dirac16c\allowbreak{}omplex}). \section{The energy-momentum tensor}

The energy-momentum tensor is defined by the vielbein variation of the action and has the closed form, for general $a_4$ and $b_4$ (\texttt{T\_vielbe\allowbreak{}in\_varia\allowbreak{}tion\_clo\allowbreak{}sed\_form\allowbreak{}\_G}; formula key \texttt{T\_variat\allowbreak{}ion}; sympy \texttt{grassman\allowbreak{}n\_emt\_eq\allowbreak{}uals\_gen\allowbreak{}eral\_vie\allowbreak{}lbein\_va\allowbreak{}riation\_\allowbreak{}on\_shell} with all 64 components of a general first-order vielbein variation)

\[
\begin{aligned}
T^\nu{}_\mu &= e^b{}_\mu\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\,\frac{\delta S}{\delta e^b{}_\nu}
= \delta^\nu_\mu\,\mathcal{L}_0 - \tfrac12\big(\bar\Psi\gamma^\nu D_\mu\Psi - D_\mu\bar\Psi\gamma^\nu\Psi\big) \\
&\quad - \tfrac14\,g_{\mu\rho}\nabla_\lambda\big(\bar\Psi\{\gamma^\lambda, \Sigma^{\nu\rho}\}\Psi\big),
\qquad \Sigma^{\nu\rho} = \tfrac14[\gamma^\nu, \gamma^\rho],\qquad \mathcal{L}_0 = \mathcal{L}/\sqrt{|g|},
\end{aligned}
\]

where the last term (the totally antisymmetric spin density) comes from the variation of the spin connection. Its symmetric part is the Belinfante tensor (\texttt{T\_symmet\allowbreak{}ric\_part\allowbreak{}\_Belinfa\allowbreak{}nte\_G}, \texttt{grassman\allowbreak{}n\_emt\_sy\allowbreak{}mmetric}; formula key \texttt{T\_symmet\allowbreak{}ric}),

\[
T^{\nu\rho} = g^{\nu\rho}\mathcal{L}_0 - \tfrac14\big(\bar\Psi\gamma^\nu D^\rho\Psi + \bar\Psi\gamma^\rho D^\nu\Psi - D^\rho\bar\Psi\gamma^\nu\Psi - D^\nu\bar\Psi\gamma^\rho\Psi\big),
\]

which equals the variation tensor on shell (section 8). Sign convention (SPEC section 4): the energy density is $\rho = -T^{x_4}{}_{x_4}$ and the pressures are $p_\mu = T^\mu{}_\mu$ (no sum) for $\mu \neq x_4$; with this convention $\rho = mS + U$ for homogeneous on-shell configurations (section 10).

Kinetic and potential parts (formula key \texttt{EMT\_kine\allowbreak{}tic\_pote\allowbreak{}ntial}): $T^\nu{}_\mu = T_{\rm kin}{}^\nu{}_\mu + T_{\rm pot}{}^\nu{}_\mu$ with

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_{\rm pot}{}^\nu{}_\mu &= -\delta^\nu_\mu\,\big(mS + U(S)\big), \\
T_{\rm kin}{}^\nu{}_\mu &= \delta^\nu_\mu\sum_\lambda K_\lambda - \tfrac14\big(\bar\Psi\gamma^\nu D_\mu\Psi - D_\mu\bar\Psi\gamma^\nu\Psi + \bar\Psi\gamma_\mu D^\nu\Psi - D^\nu\bar\Psi\gamma_\mu\Psi\big).
