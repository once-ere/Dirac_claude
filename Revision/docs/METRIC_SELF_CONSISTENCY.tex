\begin{abstract}
A diagonal metric solves the Einstein (or Einstein-Lovelock) field equations $\sum_k \alpha_k E_{(k)}{}^\mu{}_\nu + \Lambda\,\delta^\mu_\nu = \kappa\,T^\mu{}_\nu$ only if every off-diagonal component of the left-hand side equals the corresponding component of the source. The author's notebook states the same as a warning: all off-diagonal source terms must vanish for the earlier solutions for $\Psi_{16}$ to be valid. Throughout, $x_0$ is the hidden coordinate, which is space-like, and $x_4$ is the time. The variable $z = 6Hx_0$ takes every real value, and the author's $\sin^{1/3}z$ means $(\sin^2 z)^{1/6}$, the positive branch. The corrected metric that this document proposes is

\[
\begin{aligned}
ds^2 = {} & e^{-2a_4}b_4\cot^2 z\,dx_0^2 + e^{2a_4}\big(dx_1^2 + dx_2^2 + dx_3^2\big) - dx_4^2 \\
& - e^{-2a_4}b_4\,(\sin^2 z)^{1/6}\big(dx_5^2 + dx_6^2 + dx_7^2\big) .
\end{aligned}
\]

This document records what the Revision computation found.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The metric of 2026-10-14 has, at every Lovelock order, the off-diagonal component $E_{(k)}{}^{x_4}{}_{x_0} \propto \cot z\,b_4'/b_4$. In Einstein gravity it is $\frac32 H\cot z\,b_4'/b_4$, so a diagonal source forces $b_4' = 0$.
\item Version 1 of this document removed that component with $g_{44} = -(\sin^2 z)^{1/6}$. That lapse oscillates along $x_0$ and vanishes at $z = n\pi$, so version 1 is withdrawn.
\item With $g_{44} = -1$ fixed, consider the family $g_{00} = b_4^{2p}e^{2qa_4}\cot^2 z$, $g_{ii} = e^{2a_4}(\sin^2 z)^{3\alpha}$, $g_{tt} = -e^{-2a_4}b_4(\sin^2 z)^{3\beta}$. The off-diagonal terms come from mixed curvature, proportional to $\alpha\big((q-1)a_4' + pu\big)$ for 3-space and to $\beta\big((q+1)a_4' + (p-\frac12)u\big)$ for the extra times, with $u = b_4'/b_4$. The author's two warps ($\alpha = \beta = 1/6$) cannot both be kept. The only member that keeps both, $g_{00} = b_4^{1/2}\cot^2 z$, makes the Einstein tensor diagonal but not the Gauss-Bonnet tensor.
\item The corrected metric (above) keeps the warp of the extra times and gives the hidden direction the scale factor of the extra times. It has no mixed Riemann component. Every off-diagonal component of the Einstein, Gauss-Bonnet and cubic Lovelock tensors vanishes identically, for all $a_4(x_4)$, $b_4(x_4) > 0$ and every real $z$. Every diagonal component is independent of $x_0$. Each of these statements is PROVED twice, with sympy and with Wolfram.
\item The block $(x_0, x_5, x_6, x_7)$ is $e^{-2a_4}b_4$ times a 4-dimensional space of constant curvature $-H^2$, so the hidden direction shrinks together with the deflating extra times.
\item The field equations are ordinary differential equations in $x_4$. The record's history $a_4 = Hx_4$, $b_4 = e^{(4/3)Hx_4}$ needs a diagonal, conserved source. That source has $p_0 = p_t$ and grows with the magnitude $H^2e^{2a_4}/b_4 = H^2e^{(2/3)Hx_4}$ of the curvature of the shrinking block.
\item The gravity term of the Dirac operator is $\frac32 He^{a_4}b_4^{-1/2}\gamma^{(x_0)} + (b_4'/b_4 - a_4'/2)\gamma^{(x_4)}$, independent of $x_0$.
\end{enumerate}

The off-diagonal components of the energy-momentum tensor of the fields themselves (the spin-connection bilinears) remain a condition on the source. The recomputation of the field theory and of every primordial object for the corrected metric is OPEN.

\section{Where off-diagonal terms come from}

\subsection{The geometry}

Write the diagonal metric as $g = \mathrm{diag}(\eta_{\mu\mu}f_\mu^2)$, with factors that depend on $x_0$ and $x_4$ only. Every Riemann component is then of the form $R^{ab}{}_{ab}$ (a sectional curvature), except the mixed components $R^{x_0 c}{}_{x_4 c}$ and $R^{x_4 c}{}_{x_0 c}$ with $c \notin \{x_0, x_4\}$. When the factors separate, $f_c = A_c(x_4)B_c(x_0)$, the mixed component is proportional to

\[
\partial_0\ln f_c\;\big(\partial_4\ln f_c - \partial_4\ln f_0\big) - \partial_4\ln f_c\;\partial_0\ln f_4 .
\]

The off-diagonal components of every Lovelock tensor are built from these mixed components, multiplied by sectional curvatures of the remaining directions. The only ones that can be nonzero are $E_{(k)}{}^{x_4}{}_{x_0}$ and $E_{(k)}{}^{x_0}{}_{x_4}$.

In the metric of 2026-10-14 the factors are:

\begin{itemize}
\item $f_0^2 = \cot^2 z$ and $f_4 = 1$, which do not depend on the other variable;
\item $f_i = e^{a_4}(\sin^2 z)^{1/12}$ and $f_t = e^{-a_4}b_4^{1/2}(\sin^2 z)^{1/12}$, all six with $\partial_0\ln f_c = H\cot z$ for every real $z$.
\end{itemize}

The mixed component of a 3-space direction is proportional to $a_4'$, and that of an extra time to $-a_4' + u/2$. The Einstein tensor adds the six; the terms with $a_4'$ cancel and those with $u$ do not:

\[
E_{(1)}{}^{x_4}{}_{x_0} = \frac32\,H\cot z\,\frac{b_4'}{b_4} .
\]

The Gauss-Bonnet and cubic components are $\cot z\,u$ times polynomials in $a_4'$, $u$ and $H$. With the weights $\alpha_k$ the sum is $\cot z\,u\,Q$, with the record's factor $Q$. This is PROVED: the check \texttt{record\_m\allowbreak{}etric\_of\allowbreak{}fdiagona\allowbreak{}l\_reprod\allowbreak{}uced} reproduces \texttt{generalS\allowbreak{}ource.of\allowbreak{}fDiagona\allowbreak{}l\_x4x0} of the record, frozen in \texttt{Revision\allowbreak{}/metric\_\allowbreak{}warped/r\allowbreak{}eference\allowbreak{}\_2026\_10\allowbreak{}\_14/a4-o\allowbreak{}ffdiagon\allowbreak{}al-x4x0.\allowbreak{}json}, for every real $z$. A diagonal source therefore forces $u = 0$ in Einstein gravity. With the author's weights on the history $a_4 = Hx_4$ it allows only $d\ln b_4/da_4 \in \{0, 4/3\}$, the values where $Q = 0$.

\subsection{The fields}

In the record of the metric of 2026-10-14 (commit 04fa2e7: \texttt{Revision\allowbreak{}/docs/DI\allowbreak{}RAC16COM\allowbreak{}PLEX00\_F\allowbreak{}IELD\_THE\allowbreak{}ORY.md} and the textbook, chapter 9), the energy-momentum tensor of the fields has off-diagonal components of two kinds:

\begin{itemize}
\item Components with one index $x_0$ and one index $x_4$, $T^{x_4}{}_{x_0} = -\frac14(B_{40} - \cot z\,B_{04})$, built from derivatives along $x_0$ and $x_4$. They vanish for configurations that depend on $x_4$ only (check \texttt{commutin\allowbreak{}g\_T\_x4x0\allowbreak{}\_homogen\allowbreak{}eous}).
\item Components produced by the spin connection, which drops out of the Lagrangian but not out of the tensor. An example is $K^{x_4}{}_{x_1} = \frac12 e^{a_4}\sin^{1/6}z\,H\,\bar\Phi\gamma^{(4)}\gamma^{(1)}\gamma^{(0)}\Phi$ for a configuration that depends on $x_4$ only (check \texttt{spin\_con\allowbreak{}nection\_\allowbreak{}in\_the\_e\allowbreak{}nergy\_mo\allowbreak{}mentum\_t\allowbreak{}ensor}). These components belong to the source. No diagonal metric removes them. A self-consistent diagonal metric needs a source whose off-diagonal components vanish; a source without this property needs a metric that is not diagonal in the corresponding directions.

\section{The remedy: the corrected metric}

\subsection{Version 1 and why it is withdrawn}

Version 1 of this document (commit 7897829) changed one entry, $g_{44} = -1 \to -\sin^{1/3}z$, so that all seven directions $x_1, \dots, x_7$ carried the same factor; that removed every off-diagonal term. It had three faults, which the author pointed out on 2026-10-16:

\begin{itemize}
\item Its text spoke of "the time direction, which may depend on $x_0$", as if $x_0$ were time-like. In fact $x_0$ is a hypothetical space-like coordinate and $x_4$ is the time.
\item It used $z$ and the proper coordinate $y$ before defining them.
\item It restricted $z$ to $0 < z < \pi/2$.
\end{itemize}

For real $z$, $g_{44} = -(\sin^2 z)^{1/6}$ is periodic in $x_0$ with period $\pi/(6H)$, equal to $-1$ at $z = \pi/2$ and to $0$ at $z = n\pi$. The lapse of the time would oscillate along the hidden direction and vanish periodically (check \texttt{version\_\allowbreak{}1\_g44\_os\allowbreak{}cillates}), but $g_{44}$ cannot oscillate. Version 1 is withdrawn. Every metric below keeps $g_{44} = -1$.

\subsection{The family with \texorpdfstring{$g_{44} = -1$}{g\_44 = -1} and its conditions}

With $g_{44} = -1$ we have $\partial_0\ln f_4 = 0$, and the mixed component of section 2.1 reduces to $\partial_0\ln f_c\,(\partial_4\ln f_c - \partial_4\ln f_0)$. It vanishes in two cases: the direction $c$ does not depend on $x_0$, or $f_c$ changes in $x_4$ at the rate of $f_0$. Consider the family

\[
g_{00} = b_4^{2p}e^{2qa_4}\cot^2 z,\qquad g_{ii} = e^{2a_4}(\sin^2 z)^{3\alpha},\qquad g_{44} = -1,\qquad g_{tt} = -e^{-2a_4}b_4\,(\sin^2 z)^{3\beta},
\]

with real $p$, $q$, $\alpha$, $\beta$. It contains the metric of 2026-10-14 ($p = q = 0$, $\alpha = \beta = 1/6$). For every real $z$ the only mixed components are

\[
\begin{aligned}
R^{x_0 x_i}{}_{x_4 x_i} &= 18H\tan z\,e^{-2qa_4}b_4^{-2p}\;\alpha\big((q-1)a_4' + p\,u\big), \\
R^{x_0 x_t}{}_{x_4 x_t} &= 18H\tan z\,e^{-2qa_4}b_4^{-2p}\;\beta\big((q+1)a_4' + (p - \tfrac12)\,u\big).
\end{aligned}
\]

This is PROVED with Wolfram (the exact factors) and with sympy (the proportionality), in the check \texttt{family\_g\allowbreak{}44\_fixed\allowbreak{}\_conditi\allowbreak{}ons}. Both components vanish for all $a_4$, $b_4$ if and only if

\[
\big[\alpha = 0 \ \text{or}\ (q, p) = (1, 0)\big]\quad\text{and}\quad\big[\beta = 0 \ \text{or}\ (q, p) = (-1, \tfrac12)\big].
\]

So the author's two warps, $\alpha = \beta = 1/6$, cannot both be kept with vanishing mixed curvature. The Einstein tensor sees only the sum: $E_{(1)}{}^{x_4}{}_{x_0}$ is proportional to $\alpha\big((q-1)a_4' + pu\big) + \beta\big((q+1)a_4' + (p-\frac12)u\big)$ (check \texttt{family\_e\allowbreak{}instein\_\allowbreak{}conditio\allowbreak{}n}). With both warps it vanishes for all $a_4$, $b_4$ exactly when $q = 0$ and $p = 1/4$; section 3.6 shows that this member fails at the Gauss-Bonnet order.

\subsection{The corrected metric}

Keeping the warp of the extra times ($\beta = 1/6$) and dropping that of 3-space ($\alpha = 0$) gives $q = -1$, $p = 1/2$:

\[
\begin{aligned}
ds^2 = {} & e^{-2a_4}\,b_4\cot^2 z\,dx_0^2 + e^{2a_4}\big(dx_1^2 + dx_2^2 + dx_3^2\big) - dx_4^2 \\
& - e^{-2a_4}\,b_4\,(\sin^2 z)^{1/6}\big(dx_5^2 + dx_6^2 + dx_7^2\big) .
\end{aligned}
\]

This is the \textbf{corrected metric} of 2026-10-16. The functions $a_4(x_4)$ and $b_4(x_4) > 0$ are free.

\begin{itemize}
\item $g_{00} \ge 0$, $g_{ii} > 0$, $g_{44} = -1$ and $g_{tt} < 0$ for every real $z$. The signature is $(4,4)$ wherever $\sin z\cos z \neq 0$.
\item As in the author's metric, $g_{00}$ vanishes where $\cos z = 0$ and $g_{tt}$ where $\sin z = 0$. The correction adds no new degenerate points; 3-space no longer has any.
\item Compared with the metric of 2026-10-14, two entries change and the extra times stay as they were. In $g_{00} = \cot^2 z \to e^{-2a_4}b_4\cot^2 z$, the hidden direction gets the scale factor $e^{-a_4}b_4^{1/2}$ of the extra times. In $g_{ii}$, 3-space loses the factor $(\sin^2 z)^{1/6}$. $g_{44} = -1$ and $g_{tt}$ are unchanged.
\item The diagonal vielbein is $f_0 = e^{-a_4}b_4^{1/2}\cot z$ (with its sign), $f_i = e^{a_4}$, $f_4 = 1$ and $f_t = e^{-a_4}b_4^{1/2}(\sin^2 z)^{1/12}$. The volume factor is $\sqrt{|g|} = e^{-a_4}b_4^2\,|\cot z|\,(\sin^2 z)^{1/4}$ (check \texttt{correcte\allowbreak{}d\_metric\allowbreak{}\_entries}).
\item $b_4$ enters the curvature through $H^2e^{2a_4}/b_4$ as well as through $u$. Its normalization $b_4(0)$ is therefore physical in the corrected metric; the record uses $b_4(0) = 1$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The block of constant curvature}

Wherever $\sin z \neq 0$, define the proper coordinate of the hidden direction

\[
y = \frac{\ln|\sin z|}{6H} \le 0,\qquad dy = \cot z\,dx_0,\qquad (\sin^2 z)^{1/6} = e^{2Hy} .
\]

$y$ is monotonic on each interval $n\pi/2 < z < (n+1)\pi/2$. Every statement of the record is made in $x_0$, for every real $z$. In $y$ the corrected metric reads

\[
ds^2 = -dx_4^2 + e^{2a_4}\big(dx_1^2+dx_2^2+dx_3^2\big) + e^{-2a_4}b_4\,\Big(dy^2 - e^{2Hy}\big(dx_5^2+dx_6^2+dx_7^2\big)\Big).
\]

The bracket is a 4-dimensional metric of signature $(1,3)$ with constant curvature $-H^2$. Check \texttt{hidden\_b\allowbreak{}lock\_con\allowbreak{}stant\_cu\allowbreak{}rvature} shows $R^{ab}{}_{ab} = -H^2$ for its six pairs and no other component, for every real $z$. The corrected metric is therefore a product over the time line with two fibers: flat 3-space with scale factor $e^{a_4}$, and this block with scale factor $e^{-a_4}b_4^{1/2}$. A warped product over a line has no curvature components that mix the line with a fiber (B. O'Neill, \emph{Semi-Riemannian Geometry}, chapter 7), and with two fibers the same holds fiber by fiber. The block, being of constant curvature, has no mixed components of its own. The hidden direction shrinks together with the deflating extra times: on the record's history its proper size scales as $e^{-a_4}b_4^{1/2} = e^{-Hx_4/3}$.

\subsection{Every off-diagonal component vanishes}

In the corrected metric $\partial_0\ln f_i = 0$ and $\partial_4\ln f_t = \partial_4\ln f_0$, so the expression of section 2.1 vanishes for every $c$. There is no mixed Riemann component at all: check \texttt{correcte\allowbreak{}d\_mixed\_\allowbreak{}riemann\_\allowbreak{}vanish} finds 28 nonzero components, all of the form $R^{ab}{}_{ab}$. The off-diagonal components of every Lovelock tensor are built from mixed components, so all 56 off-diagonal components of $E_{(1)}$, $E_{(2)}$ and $E_{(3)}$ vanish identically, for all $a_4(x_4)$, $b_4(x_4) > 0$, $H$ and every real $z$. Every diagonal component is independent of $x_0$ (check \texttt{correcte\allowbreak{}d\_offdia\allowbreak{}gonal\_al\allowbreak{}l\_orders}, sympy and Wolfram). For a diagonal source that depends on $x_4$ only, the field equations are therefore ordinary differential equations in $x_4$, at every Lovelock order.

\subsection{The alternatives that were not chosen}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Alternative K.} It keeps the warp of 3-space instead ($\alpha = 1/6$, $\beta = 0$, so $q = 1$, $p = 0$): $g_{00} = e^{2a_4}\cot^2 z$, $g_{ii} = e^{2a_4}(\sin^2 z)^{1/6}$, $g_{tt} = -e^{-2a_4}b_4$. \section{The field equations of the corrected metric}

\subsection{The Einstein tensor}

The Ricci tensor is diagonal and independent of $x_0$ (check \texttt{ricci\_te\allowbreak{}nsor}, sympy and Wolfram). Write $C = H^2e^{2a_4}/b_4$; at each time the hidden block has the constant curvature $-C$. The Einstein tensor is

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{(1)}{}^{x_4}{}_{x_4} &= 6C + 3a_4'^2 - \tfrac32 u^2, \\
E_{(1)}{}^{x_i}{}_{x_i} &= 6C - 5a_4'^2 + 6a_4'u + 2a_4'' - 2\frac{b_4''}{b_4} - \tfrac12 u^2, \\
E_{(1)}{}^{x_0}{}_{x_0} = E_{(1)}{}^{x_t}{}_{x_t} &= 3C - 3a_4'^2 + \tfrac32 a_4'u - \tfrac32\frac{b_4''}{b_4},
\end{aligned}
\]

and the Ricci scalar is $R = -12C + 8a_4'^2 - 2a_4'' - 8a_4'u + 4b_4''/b_4 + u^2$. These are the keys \texttt{einstein\allowbreak{}\_diagona\allowbreak{}l} and \texttt{ricci\_sc\allowbreak{}alar\_mat\allowbreak{}hematica} of the results file \texttt{warped-m\allowbreak{}etric.js\allowbreak{}on}. The hidden direction and the extra times have equal components, because they form one block. The Gauss-Bonnet and cubic components are diagonal and independent of $x_0$ as well (section 3.5).

\subsection{Conservation}

For a diagonal tensor $T = \mathrm{diag}(p_0, p_3, p_3, p_3, -\rho, p_t, p_t, p_t)$ whose entries depend on $x_0$ and $x_4$, the corrected metric gives (check \texttt{divergen\allowbreak{}ce\_of\_di\allowbreak{}agonal\_t\allowbreak{}ensor})

\[
\begin{aligned}
\nabla_\mu T^\mu{}_{x_4} &= -\partial_4\rho - 3a_4'(\rho + p_3) - 3h(\rho + p_t) - h(\rho + p_0),\qquad h = \frac{b_4'}{2b_4} - a_4', \\
\nabla_\mu T^\mu{}_{x_0} &= \partial_0 p_0 + 3H\cot z\,(p_0 - p_t),
\end{aligned}
\]

and the six other components vanish identically. Each expanding direction dilutes $\rho$ plus its own pressure, at the rate $a_4'$ for 3-space and $h$ for the block. For entries independent of $x_0$, the $x_0$ balance requires $p_0 = p_t$.

\subsection{The record's history and its source}

\section{What the corrected metric changes for the fields}

\subsection{The vielbein, the volume and the gravity term}

With the vielbein of section 3.3, the gravity term of the Dirac operator, $\gamma^\mu\Omega_\mu = \sum_a\gamma^{(a)}\frac{1}{2V}\partial_a\big(V/f_a\big)$ with $V = f_0 f_1\cdots f_7$, is

\[

for every real $z$ (check \texttt{dirac\_gr\allowbreak{}avity\_te\allowbreak{}rm}, sympy and Wolfram). In the metric of 2026-10-14 it was $3H\gamma^{(x_0)} + \frac34(b_4'/b_4)\gamma^{(x_4)}$. On the record's history it is $\frac32 He^{Hx_4/3}\gamma^{(x_0)} + \frac56 H\gamma^{(x_4)}$: the hidden coefficient grows as the hidden direction shrinks. The record proves, for every diagonal vielbein, that $\{\gamma^\mu, \Omega_\mu\} = 0$ holds for each direction separately, which still removes the spin connection from the Lagrangian.

\subsection{Configurations that depend on \texorpdfstring{$x_4$}{x\_4} only}

Both coefficients of the gravity term, and the derivative term $\gamma^{(x_4)}\partial_4$, are independent of $x_0$. For a configuration $\Phi(x_4)$ the field equation is therefore an ordinary differential equation in $x_4$ for every mass $m$ and coupling $\lambda$; the obstruction that version 1 produced, with condensates only at $m = \pm\frac72 H$, does not arise. The solutions, the energy-momentum tensor and the quantisation are recomputed in the field-theory record. At this commit that recomputation is OPEN.

\subsection{The field's own off-diagonal components}

The spin connection of the corrected metric is not zero. For a diagonal vielbein, $\Omega_{x_c}$ is a sum of terms $\partial_b f_c/f_b\;\gamma^{(c)}\gamma^{(b)}$, and with the vielbein of section 3.3 (derived here; the field-theory record recomputes it):

\begin{itemize}
\item $\Omega_{x_i}$ is proportional to $a_4'\gamma^{(i)}\gamma^{(4)}$;
\item $\Omega_{x_t}$ has a term in $\gamma^{(t)}\gamma^{(4)}$ and one in $\gamma^{(t)}\gamma^{(0)}$;
\item $\Omega_{x_0}$ is proportional to $\gamma^{(0)}\gamma^{(4)}$;
\item $\Omega_{x_4} = 0$, because $f_4 = 1$.
\end{itemize}

Its bilinears enter the energy-momentum tensor as in section 2.2. A self-consistent source must have vanishing off-diagonal components.
